## context (YT only)

Your body runs on an internal clock. During the day, it's ready to handle food. At night, it's getting ready to rest and repair, not to digest a big plate of noodles. Your body also handles sugar better earlier in the day. The same meal at night can lead to a bigger blood sugar spike than it would at lunch.

## point1 (YT only)

Lie down right after a big meal, and stomach acid can creep back up. [sighs] That's the burning feeling called heartburn. Doctors often suggest waiting a few hours after eating before you go to bed. And a heavy late meal can make sleep worse. Your body is busy digesting when it should be winding down, so you might wake up more and feel tired the next day. Spicy, fatty, and big meals are the usual troublemakers.

## point2 (SHORT + YT)

[surprised] Here's the big one. In a controlled study, people ate the exact same food, just later in the day. Eating late made them hungrier, changed their hunger hormones, and they burned a bit fewer calories. The researchers think late eating could make it harder to manage weight over time, even without eating more.
