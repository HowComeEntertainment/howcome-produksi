Quick question. Are you blinking right now? Now you're thinking about it. But what if you stopped blinking completely? Even for a minute?

You blink around fifteen to twenty times a minute without even noticing. That's thousands of blinks a day. Each one spreads a thin layer of tears across your eyes, like a tiny windshield wiper. Blinking also sweeps away dust, and it takes only about a tenth of a second. It happens so fast that your brain hides the darkness from you.

Stop blinking, and within seconds that tear layer starts breaking up. Your eyes sting, burn, and turn red. Keep going, and the surface of your eye gets dry and irritated. It can even get damaged over time. Your eyes would also lose their protection. Dust and germs would stay on the surface instead of getting washed away. That's why people who can't fully close their eyes need special drops and care from doctors.

And here's the thing. You're already blinking way less right now. Studies found people blink much less when they stare at screens. That's one big reason your eyes feel tired and dry after hours on your phone. In one study, people's blink rate dropped from about eighteen times a minute to just a few while working on a computer.

Plot twist! Blinking isn't just for your eyes. In one study, scientists saw that right after people blink, the brain's resting network lights up for a moment. Every blink might be a tiny mental break. So if you've been staring at your phone, try the twenty-twenty-twenty rule. Every twenty minutes, look at something twenty feet away for twenty seconds.
