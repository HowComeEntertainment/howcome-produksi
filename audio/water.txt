No soda. No juice. No sweet coffee. Only water. What if you drank nothing but water for thirty days? Sounds boring, right?

For a lot of people, sugary drinks are the biggest source of added sugar in their whole diet. One regular soda can have around thirty-nine grams of sugar. That's almost ten teaspoons, gone in a few minutes. And sweet coffee drinks, juices, and energy drinks can be just as sugary. A lot of people drink their sugar without ever noticing it.

Week one might feel weird. If you drank a lot of soda or coffee, you could get headaches and cravings. But your teeth get a break from sugar and acid baths all day long, and your body gets a break from those sugar spikes. By week two, those cravings usually calm down. Many people say plain water starts tasting better, and fruit suddenly tastes sweeter. Your pee also tells the story: pale yellow means you're drinking enough.

Here's what surprises people. Liquid calories don't fill you up the way food does. So when you swap sugary drinks for water, you can cut hundreds of calories a day, without eating any less food. Studies that swapped sugary drinks for water found people often ate fewer calories overall and some lost a little weight.

Plot twist! You can actually drink too much water. Chug huge amounts in a short time, and the salt in your blood can drop dangerously low. It's rare, but it happens. Your kidneys can only handle so much at once. And no, you don't need to force eight huge glasses if you're not thirsty. Food gives you water too.
