Bzzz. You grab your phone. Nothing. Five minutes later, again. What if you checked your phone every five minutes, all day long? Sound familiar?

Honestly, that's not far from normal. One big survey found Americans check their phones about ninety-six times a day. That's roughly once every ten minutes you're awake. And most of the time, you don't even remember doing it. Some people check way more than that, especially teens.

Every check pulls your focus away. And getting back into deep focus isn't instant. Researchers who study work interruptions found it can take a long time to fully get back on track. So a day full of tiny checks becomes a day without real focus. Every buzz also gives your brain a tiny jolt of something new, which is exactly why it's so hard to ignore. And checking in bed? The bright screen keeps your brain alert and can push your sleep later.

Here's the wild part. In one experiment, people did harder thinking tests just because their phone was sitting on the desk. Turned off, face down, didn't matter. Phone in another room? They scored better. Just having it nearby seems to use up some brainpower, because part of you is busy trying not to check it.

Plot twist! Ever felt your phone buzz, but it didn't? That's called phantom vibration. In one study of hospital staff, most of them had felt it. Your brain is so tuned to your phone, it starts imagining it. Some people even feel it buzzing in a pocket where the phone isn't.
