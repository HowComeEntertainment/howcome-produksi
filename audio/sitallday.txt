You sit for breakfast, sit in the car, sit at your desk, then sit on the couch to scroll. What happens if you sit all day, every day?

The average American adult sits about six and a half hours a day. Teens? More than eight. People even call sitting the new smoking.

Your leg muscles work like sugar sponges. When they move, they pull sugar out of your blood. Sit for hours, and those sponges basically fall asleep. Your hips get tight, and your lower back starts complaining.

Here's the scary part. After just three hours of sitting, scientists measured worse blood flow in the leg arteries. But a five-minute walk every hour kept it normal.

Plot twist! Sitting is NOT the new smoking. Smoking is way worse. A study of over a million people found that about an hour of brisk walking a day seemed to cancel out the extra risk of sitting eight hours.
